CreativeArtist

A-MASHIRO

SAKAZUKIbringspeopletogether.

A-Mashiro is a Tokyo-based artist who has been creating works for over 20 years. Born in a traditional Japanese house with over 200 years of history, he grew up immersed in traditional Japanese culture and nature. This rich natural environment profoundly influenced his creativity, an influence strongly evident in his current work. While he initially worked with oil and acrylic paints, his current practice centers on digital art. His early style was driven by emotion, but he gradually shifted focus to composition and color balance. This shift reflects his view that creation is also a means of maintaining balance between mind and body. He also has deep connections with generative artists, establishing a unique position among 1/1 artists.

FOUNDER

HIGHBALLCharlie

FOUNDER

Hi(ball)

“Sakazuki” means “sake cup” in Japan. People used to clink these small cups to show trust, respect, and friendship. This is more than drinking! It’s a simple act that brings people closer and opens the heart. No other creature shares a cup to connect like this. Only humans do. And that’s why, for me, the Sakazuki is one of the purest symbols of what it means to be human.

Biography

Charlie has been with Suntory for 27 years and is known as the “Father of the Highball,” having sparked Japan’s Highball boom. He developed both the Highball mug and the Highball machine, and is the founder of Good Measure, a Web3 startup within the Suntory Group.

FAMILY

TONY

Immigrant activist turned entrepreneur in the cryptocurrency, blockchain, and NFT space. He is a founding member of prominent DAOs, such as NeonDAO, ReadyPlayerDAO, BrightMomentsDAO, and MeebitsDAO. Tony is an active member of the Web3 community, mentoring and consulting in an effort to support growth in the space.tonyherrera.com

HARRY

Harry has been a project manager in the crypto/NFT space since 2021, working in everything from fine art to pure degen projects. Having lived and worked in Japan and for Japanese companies he also brings a wealth of experience in introducing the hidden gems of Japan to first time and repeat visitors.

STEVE

Stephen McKeon is a managing partner at Collab+Currency, a frontier technology venture fund that has completed over 150 deals across nearly every corner of crypto. They focus primarily on the intersection of AI and decentralized systems. Previously, he was a tenured finance professor at the University of Oregon and co-founded Skyward, a drone software startup.

NEORT

NEORT is supporting the connection between artists who use digital technology and society. In April 2022, NEORT++ an exhibition space specializing in digital art opened in Bakurocho. We aim to create new art experiences between online and offline.

FAQ

QUESTION
01
What currency can I use to purchase Sakazuki Collective(NFT)?

A. You can purchase it using the cryptocurrency Ethereum or a credit card.

QUESTION
02
Is Sakazuki Collective (NFT) an SBT (Soulbound Token)?

A. No. It is a transferable token.

QUESTION
03
Do I purchase the Japan Experience pass and Rare Creations as NFTs?

A. Yes. You purchase them as NFTs, and after redemption you can arrange travel bookings or request product fulfillment online.

QUESTION
04
When will the schedule for Japan Experience and Global Gathering be announced?

A. We will publish an annual calendar when the website is relaunched.

QUESTION
05
Does my "SAKAZUKI Collective (NFT)" expire?

A. No — membership is lifelong as long as you hold your pass. A yearly calendar helps you plan your Japan experiences, which must be purchased separately.

QUESTION
06
How do I purchase Japan Experiences or Rare Creations?

A. Your SAKAZUKI Collective (NFT) unlocks access on the Sakazuki website. From there, you may purchase Japan Experience Passes or member-only creations. The website is currently being rebuilt and will relaunch in March/April 2026.

QUESTION
07
Can I purchase rare bottles through SAKAZUKI?

A. Yes — some rare whiskies may require completion of specific experiences. All alcohol bottle pickups occur in Japan in accordance with local regulations.

QUESTION
08
Do I need to buy a pass for Global Gatherings?

A. Depends on the event — some are free, others paid. Members who complete specific experiences may receive free access as a reward.

QUESTION
09
What does "SAKAZUKI" mean in Japanese?

A. "Sakazuki" means a "sake cup" in Japan. Traditionally, people would clink these small cups as a symbol of trust, respect, and friendship. In Japan, exchanging sakazuki cups in traditional wedding ceremonies represents an "eternal bond."
